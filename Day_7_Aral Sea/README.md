# 🛰️ Day 07: 10-Minute Map - The Aral Sea from Space (2000 vs. 2026)

A rapid remote-sensing comparison capturing 26 years of dramatic environmental transformation across the **Aral Sea** in Karakalpakstan, Uzbekistan, and Kazakhstan, created in under 10 minutes using **NASA Worldview** 🇺🇿✨

---

## 📊 Technical Workflow & Remote Sensing Details

### 1. Satellite Imagery & Sensor Specifications
* **Platform & Tool:** Generated directly via **NASA Worldview** (`worldview.earthdata.nasa.gov`).
* **Sensor Data:** Utilized **NASA Terra / MODIS Corrected Reflectance (True Color)** daily imagery at a spatial resolution of **250 meters** per pixel.

### 2. Temporal & Spatial Alignment
* **Cloud-Free Selection:** Isolated clear summer passes on **August 28, 2000** and **August 10, 2026** to maximize surface visibility.
* **Exact Bounding Box:** Locked identical geographic coordinates (`Top Right: 46.9°N, 61.9°E` to `Bottom Left: 43.3°N, 57.9°E`) to export aligned `1257 × 1132` pixel snapshots.
* **Interactive Analysis:** Explored multi-temporal changes using Worldview's interactive **Swipe**, **Opacity**, and **Spy** comparison modes.

---

## 🌍 Key Geographic & Environmental Observations
* **Birth of the Aralkum Desert:** The vast eastern basin of the South Aral Sea seen in 2000 has dried up, leaving behind bright white salt flats and sand known as the **Aralkum**.
* **Western Basin Remnant:** Only a narrow strip of dark water remains along the steep eastern cliffs of the Ustyurt Plateau.
* **Northern Sea Recovery:** The Small (North) Aral Sea in Kazakhstan shows visible expansion in 2026 following the completion of the **Kokaral Dam** in 2005, which retains Syr Darya river water.
* **Amu Darya Delta:** Lush green irrigated agricultural fields and wetlands remain visible at the southern edge in Karakalpakstan.
